With Julien Schuh.

This talk explores the use of generative LLMs and multi-agent systems to interpret literary texts, focusing on a corpus of crime novels gathered as part of the ANR POLARisation project. Rather than limiting itself to classical quantitative approaches, the study proposes an experimental methodology in which AI agents, able to communicate with one another and to learn over the course of their readings, recreate and simulate the reception mechanisms of model readers.

We aim to use several LLM agents jointly — generative or not — each with a memory in the form of an external database, and each with an associated sub-task that builds on previous results to serve their ultimate common goal: interpreting a novel and situating it within a typology. This protocol interrogates the interpretive creativity of machines, with implications for literary research, reception theory and the reactivation of older corpora.
