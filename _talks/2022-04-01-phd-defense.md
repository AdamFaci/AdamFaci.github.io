Doctoral defence before a jury composed of Jean-Gabriel Ganascia, Cyril de Runz, Trevor Martin, Maria Rifqi, Marie-Jeanne Lesot and Claire Laudy. The degree of *docteur en informatique* of Sorbonne Université was awarded unanimously.

The thesis is organised around three contributions: an algorithm for mining frequent conceptual graph patterns using symbolic information fusion operators; a generator of synthetic conceptual graph databases used to validate that extraction; and a critical review of the introduction of fuzzy modalities into conceptual graphs.

The thesis addresses knowledge representation in conceptual graphs, a structured knowledge representation formalism that provides efficient manipulation tools. Conceptual graphs, by their ability to visually represent different kinds of knowledge — mainly ontological and factual — and by offering efficient manipulation tools, provide a framework for the concise representation of large amounts of information. The work conducts a comparative study of fuzzy extensions of conceptual graphs, then proposes an algorithm for conceptual graph simulation as well as an efficient algorithm for extracting frequent patterns that are not redundant with ontological knowledge.
