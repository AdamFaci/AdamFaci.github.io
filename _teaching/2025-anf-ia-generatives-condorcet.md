National training school (*Action Nationale de Formation*) for laboratory staff on working with generative AI, given as a feedback report on adapting AI to the actual needs of SSH researchers &mdash; drawing on work carried out at the HN Lab with Léa Maronet, Stéphane Pouyllau, Émile Provendier, Antoine Silvestre de Sacy, Julien Schuh, Camille Bouzereau and Irène Langlet.

The session is organised discipline by discipline rather than technique by technique, so that each method arrives attached to the research question that motivated it.

Outline
======

**1. AI as a change of space.** The working parts of a transformer &mdash; tokenisation, word embeddings, attention &mdash; and their vision counterparts: image tokenisation and image attention. What it means, concretely, to turn a text or an image into coordinates.

**2. AI for art history.** SegmentArt, with Léa Maronet, and ModoAp, by Julien Schuh. An annotation chain for recognising and classifying plant and animal motifs on photographs of ancient Indian sculpture (3rd c. BCE to 3rd c. CE), built with YOLOv9 on Teklia's Arkindex platform &mdash; and its limits: annotation is extremely time-consuming when precise masks are needed, model choice is restricted, semantic levels are not accounted for, and the black-box aspect leaves little control over parameters, scores and outputs. The session then moves to combining models (Grounding DINO + SAM 2) on the Élie Kagan photographic collection, and towards richer annotation through knowledge graphs, in the manner of Visual Genome. The underlying difficulty is the semantic gap: the distance between the object represented and any description of it, which is always a particular cultural reading of the image.

**3. AI for linguistics.** *La gifle en motifs*, with Camille Bouzereau: textual sequences around the stem *gifl-* across a corpus of French crime fiction, from the raw concordance to the recurrent patterns through which female bodies are represented.

**4. The latest LLM innovation: RAG.** A review of complex retrieval-augmented generation architectures &mdash; Self-RAG, Clustered RAG, Multi-Head RAG, RAPTOR, Corrective RAG, Speculative RAG, Chain-of-Note and GraphRAG, with its global, local and DRIFT search modes.

**5. AI for sociology.** *Verlan2000*, with Émile Provendier.

**6. AI for literature.** Towards a RAG algorithm adapted to the interpretation of texts, using anchoring elements such as conjugation, punctuation and style.
