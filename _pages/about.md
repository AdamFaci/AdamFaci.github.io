I am a postdoctoral researcher at [Huma-Num](https://www.huma-num.fr/) (UAR 3598, CNRS), the French national research infrastructure for the digital humanities, where I work in the HN Lab on **large language models and knowledge graphs for the social sciences and humanities (SSH)**.

I hold a PhD in Artificial Intelligence from Sorbonne Université (LIP6, 2022), on the representation, simulation and mining of knowledge in the **conceptual graph** formalism. Since then my work has moved from purely symbolic knowledge representation towards **neuro-symbolic and interdisciplinary methods**: how generative models can be made controllable, documentable and interpretable enough to be used as research instruments by historians, literary scholars, linguists and social scientists — and how the epistemological assumptions built into those models reshape the disciplines that adopt them.

Research interests
======

* **Retrieval-augmented generation and scholarly agents.** Making LLM outputs traceable back to a controlled corpus, and routing research questions to explicit, inspectable plans rather than to a single opaque generation. I lead the *ReSearch SSH* use case of the European project **LLMs4EU**, which aims at multilingual, interpretable LLM-based research assistants for the SSH.
* **Knowledge graphs and neuro-symbolic methods.** Conceptual graphs, ontologies, pattern mining, and the combination of symbolic structure with learned representations — including the use of ontological "lenses" over conversational agents.
* **Computer vision for art history and photojournalism.** Frugal segmentation and clustering pipelines (SAM2, DINO, YOLO) for large image corpora, and the epistemological cost of reducing an expert description to a single annotation label. See the [SegmentArt](https://github.com/AdamFaci/segmentart) tool.
* **Evaluation, bias and research ethics.** Benchmarking language models on scholarly SSH literature, measuring identity bias in LLM-generated peer review, and writing practical guidelines for SSH communities.
* **Socio-history of AI in the humanities.** How AI methods and media discourse on AI have circulated through French digital humanities since 2000, and unevenly across disciplines.

I also teach and run training sessions on AI for humanities audiences — from master's seminars to CNRS national training schools — and I take that teaching to be part of the research rather than a by-product of it: the classroom is where the methodological assumptions get tested.
