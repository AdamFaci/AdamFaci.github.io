Computational art history routinely takes digitised collections as data and their catalogue records as ground truth. But a catalogue is an argument: it encodes a period's classification decisions, its silences, and its institutional priorities, and a model trained or evaluated on it reproduces those decisions as if they were properties of the images.

This paper makes the catalogue an explicit object of study within the pipeline, and shows what changes in the results when archival categories are treated as a variable rather than as a label.
