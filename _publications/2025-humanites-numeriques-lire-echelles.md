In this article, we propose a research methodology that combines retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) approaches with computational processing. This approach is applied to the study of a corpus of art history journals and the analysis of a fundamental and controversial concept: style.

Through this example, we explore the challenges associated with integrating these methods into the humanities, highlighting their potential contributions while underlining their limitations. Far from considering artificial intelligence as a universal solution or an end in itself, our methodology instead calls for a critical appropriation of these tools by researchers. It emphasises the need to adapt them to the specific issues of the disciplines involved, through differentiated methodological frameworks applied to familiar and well-understood corpora, while ensuring the explicability of the results.
