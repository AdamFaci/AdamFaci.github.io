An ongoing strand of work on the evaluation of discriminatory bias in generative language models, and on turning that evaluation into guidelines that SSH communities can apply.

It has two sides. The measurement side audits LLM-assisted scholarly evaluation under controlled conditions: the scientific content of a manuscript is held constant while who is said to have written it, where, and in which language vary. A first poster with Alice Breton, [*Writing and Evaluating Under an Identity*](/talks/2026-09-03-mashs-identity-bias-peer-review), presented how an author's stated gender and affiliation shift the reviews a model generates for an otherwise identical manuscript. Two papers in preparation extend the audit: [*The Geography and Language of Legitimacy*](/publications/2026-cl-geography-language-legitimacy/) to peer review across institutions and languages, and [*The Model as Gatekeeper*](/publications/2027-acl-model-as-gatekeeper/) to the editorial tasks around it.

The prescriptive side turns those findings into recommendations usable by laboratories and infrastructures, and feeds directly into the [training sessions](/teaching/) I run for CNRS and Huma-Num.
